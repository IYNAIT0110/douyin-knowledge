## B4. 稳定：为什么"拼命"恰恰是错的姿势（283–401s）

## B5. 单目标：一个结果如何替你筛选掉十个愿望（401–518s）

**EN**
Why does a thirty-day plan insist on chasing exactly one result? Because stalled goals are rarely a difficulty problem; they are a bandwidth problem. Want to get fit, learn a language, grow an account, level up at work, start reading, improve your social life — every item matters, so every item receives a sliver of attention, and everything stays permanently in the state of "just getting started". The fix is to elect one primary goal, not ten wishes: a result whose progress can be judged clearly — finishing a portfolio, mastering one core skill, clearing one stage of an exam, laying the base of a long-term project, or finally killing a problem that has been dragged around for ages. A single goal then does quiet, mechanical work for you: it filters. Once you know what these thirty days are really for, it gets easier to tell what is worth doing from what merely looks busy. And a subtle psychological bonus follows: with fewer choices, internal friction drops, because you stop re-deciding your direction every morning. Real execution is not daily enthusiasm; it is still knowing what to do on the days when the excitement is gone. Thirty days of chasing one core result looks simple, yet it gives your actions something they never had — depth.

> **中**
> 为什么三十天计划坚持只追一个结果？因为迟迟没有结果的目标，很少是"太难"的问题，而是带宽的问题。想减脂、想学语言、想做账号、想提升工作能力、想开始阅读、还想改善社交——每一件都重要，于是每一件都只分到一点点注意力，所有事情都永远停在"正在开始"的状态。解法是选出一个主目标，而不是十个愿望：一个可以清晰判断进展的结果——完成一个作品集、掌握一项核心技能、过掉某个考试阶段、搭好一个长期项目的地基，或者把一个拖了很久的问题真正解决掉。然后，这个唯一的目标会替你做安静的、机械的活儿：筛选。一旦你知道这三十天到底是为了什么，判断"什么值得做"和"什么只是看起来很忙"就容易多了。还有一个不易察觉的心理红利：选择变少之后，内耗反而下降，因为你不再每天早上重新决定自己该往哪走。真正的执行力不是每天满怀激情，而是在激情退场的那些天里，仍然知道自己该做什么。三十天只追一个核心结果，看起来简单，却让你的行动第一次拥有了它从未有过的东西——纵深。

## B6. 修正引擎：反馈速度才是成长速度（518–629s）

**EN**
The last stretch turns motivation into mechanics, and it is the part most self-improvement advice leaves out. Plain repetition does not automatically produce progress — plenty of people work hard for months while quietly repeating the same error. ## C2. Orbit Changes and Self-Trust

## C3. Focus as an Environment Problem

*Terms: **on purpose** = deliberately, not by accident; **warm-up** = the slow ramp before real work begins; **deflationary** = making something sound less grand than people assume.*

Read the whole twelve minutes as one argument and a single absence stands out: it never once asks you to try harder. Every instruction is subtractive or environmental. Focus is defined as putting yourself inside surroundings that favour success; the price of focus is described as comfort you hand over in advance; the discipline that gets praised is the discipline of muting notifications and going to bed on time, not the discipline of suffering; the goal is narrowed to one so that decisions stop being made every morning; and the metric is moved from hours logged to corrections logged. Even the closing image is environmental — two people start from an almost invisible difference in how they spend attention, and time merely magnifies it. That is the transferable claim hiding under the motivational wrapping: if focus keeps failing, the fault is usually in the setup, not in the willpower. Change the environment, shrink the number of decisions, shorten the feedback cycle — and the intensity people try so hard to summon becomes a by-product.
