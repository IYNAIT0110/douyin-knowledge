# Part A · 核心论点（逐句对照）
# Part A · Core Arguments (Sentence by Sentence)

## A1. 三步不是清单，是淘汰赛 / Three Steps, but Only One of Them Is Defensible

**EN** Everyone will end up with the same tools and the same models, so the difference has to come from somewhere else.
> **中** 最后大家的工具是一样的，大家的模型是一样的，所以差距只能来自别的地方。

**EN** The first step is to stop using ChatGPT and start using an agent.
> **中** 第一步是停止使用 ChatGPT，开始使用 Agent。

**EN** The second step is to write a proper prompt.
> **中** 第二步是写好一个 Prompt。

**EN** The third step is to accumulate as much context as you can, and then figure out how to distill it.
> **中** 第三步是尽可能多地积累上下文，然后再想办法把它提炼好。

**EN** The first two steps spread like information; the third one only grows with time.
> **中** 前两步像信息一样传播，第三步只能靠时间长出来。

**EN** Anything that spreads like information gets copied within weeks, so it cannot be your edge.
> **中** 凡是像信息一样传播的东西，几周内就会被抄走，所以它不可能成为你的优势。

## A2. Prompt 不是文风，是接口规范 / A Prompt Is an Interface, Not a Writing Style

**EN** A lot of people have turned the prompt into something purely about style.
> **中** 现在很多人把 Prompt 写成了一个纯风格向的东西。

**EN** You see prompts that ask for a Socratic tone, or for a calm and reasonable tone, and that is all they ask for.
> **中** 你会看到那种要求"苏格拉底式语气"或者"理性平和语气"的 Prompt，而它们要求的就只有这些。

**EN** A real prompt is tied to your workflow, to the tools you can call, and to the standard you are holding the output to.
> **中** 真正有效的 Prompt，是跟流程、跟你能调的工具、跟你判定输出的标准绑在一起的。

**EN** A style instruction tells the model how to sound; an interface instruction tells it what to do.
> **中** 风格指令告诉模型该怎么说话，接口指令告诉它该做什么。

**EN** Before you ship a prompt, check it for three things: a process, a tool, and a standard.
> **中** 一条 Prompt 定稿前，检查它有没有三样东西：流程、工具、标准。

**EN** If it has none of the three, you are not writing a prompt, you are just adjusting the tone.
> **中** 如果三样一样都没有，那你不是在写 Prompt，你只是在调语气。

## A3. 积累和提炼必须分开做 / Accumulating and Distilling Are Two Separate Jobs

**EN** Accumulating context and distilling context are two separate steps, and the order matters.
> **中** 积累上下文和提炼上下文是两个分开的步骤，而且顺序很重要。

**EN** You can start accumulating today, and you do not need to know how you will distill any of it yet.
> **中** 你今天就可以开始积累，暂时完全不用管将来怎么提炼。

**EN** People usually do it backwards: they design a perfect folder structure first and then wait for material to fill it.
> **中** 人们通常是反着来的：先设计一套完美的目录结构，然后等着材料往里填。

**EN** The structure you design before you have any material describes the work you imagine, not the work you actually do.
> **中** 在你还没有任何材料时设计出来的结构，描述的是你想象中的工作，不是你真实在做的工作。

**EN** Accumulating is irreversible and distilling is not, so always do the irreversible one first.
> **中** 积累是不可逆的，提炼是可逆的，所以永远先做不可逆的那一个。

## A4. 为什么要提炼：因为塞不下 / Why Distill at All: Because You Cannot Feed It Everything

**EN** You cannot hand the model every piece of context for every single task.
> **中** 你不可能把所有上下文、在每一个任务上都塞给 AI。

**EN** What you need is for the model to know which context this particular task actually calls for.
> **中** 你需要的是让 AI 聪明地知道，眼前这个任务到底需要哪一部分上下文。

**EN** That makes it a retrieval problem, not a storage problem.
> **中** 所以这是一个检索问题，不是存储问题。

**EN** The point of distilling is not to make the pile smaller; it is to make each piece findable.
> **中** 提炼的目的不是把体积缩小，而是让每一块都能被准确取出来。

## A5. 复利：他给的是证据，不是鸡汤 / Compounding: He Offers Evidence, Not Encouragement

**EN** I can feel very clearly that this has been compounding for me.
> **中** 我现在就能很明显感觉到，这件事对我是有复利的。

**EN** Give me any corporate training request, however demanding, and I am confident I can deliver it within two days.
> **中** 随便给我多高的企业培训要求，我都有信心在两天之内交付。

**EN** Not because I got better at prompting, but because the context has piled up thick enough that I already know what they want.
> **中** 不是因为我更会写 Prompt 了，而是因为上下文已经堆得足够厚，我已经知道他们要什么。

**EN** A competitor can buy the same model and copy the same prompt, but they cannot buy the year of context you already have.
> **中** 竞争对手可以买同样的模型、抄同样的 Prompt，但他买不到你已经攒下的那一年上下文。

# Part B · 论证展开（整段英文 → 整段中文）
# Part B · The Argument in Full (English Paragraph First)

## B1. The Three Steps Shrink in How Copyable They Are

**EN**
Read the three steps as a ranking, not a checklist, and they start to make much more sense. Step one is a purchasing decision: swap a chat window for an agent. It costs almost nothing to copy, which is exactly why it will be table stakes within a year. Step two is a document: someone writes a good prompt, posts it, and ten thousand people have it by lunch. Step three is different in kind, because the input is not knowledge but elapsed time, and elapsed time cannot be transmitted. That difference gives you a rule for allocating your own effort. Advice that lives in information inflates away — learn it late and you lose, but once it is public everyone is equal again. Advice that lives in time does not inflate: starting today and starting next year differ by exactly one year, and that year is the whole asset. So when someone hands you five recommendations, sort them by this one question and put your hours at the bottom of the list.

> **中**
> 把这三步读成一个排序、而不是一张清单，它们一下子就说得通了。第一步是一次采购决策：把聊天窗口换成智能体。它几乎没有复制成本，所以不出一年就会变成人人都有。第二步是一份文档：有人写了一条好 Prompt 发出来，到午饭时就有一万个人拿到了。第三步则在性质上不同，因为它的投入不是知识而是流逝的时间，而流逝的时间是没法传递的。这个差别给了你一条分配精力的规则：活在信息里的建议会通货膨胀 —— 知道得晚你会吃亏，但一旦公开，大家又平等了；活在时间里的建议不会通胀 —— 今天开始和明年开始，差的正好是一年，而那一年就是全部资产。所以别人递给你五条建议时，就按这一条问题排序，然后把时间花在榜单最下面那几条上。

## B2. Why "You Do Not Need to Know How to Distill Yet" Is the Hardest Advice Here

**EN**
The instruction to start accumulating without any plan for distilling it sounds careless, and it is the opposite. Distilling is a compression method, and a compression method has to fit the material it compresses. If you design the scheme first, you are designing it against your idea of your work rather than against your actual work, and the moment reality disagrees you will spend more time maintaining the scheme than using it. There is also an asymmetry in the cost of getting it wrong. A bad filing system can be thrown out and rebuilt on a Saturday, because the raw material is still sitting there. Material you never captured is simply gone — the conversation you had three months ago, the reason a decision was made, the thing a client said they hated. One failure is recoverable and the other is not, and the recoverable one is the one people obsess over first. So do the unglamorous version: dump everything in now, and let the structure emerge from the pile later.

> **中**
> "先开始积累、暂时不用管怎么提炼"这句话听起来很草率，实际上恰恰相反。提炼是一种压缩方法，而压缩方法必须适配它所压缩的材料。先设计分类方案，等于在拿你对自己工作的想象来设计，而不是拿你真实的工作来设计；一旦现实和想象冲突，你花在维护这套方案上的时间会超过用它省下的时间。而且两种错误的代价是不对称的：一套糟糕的归档系统可以在某个周六推倒重来，因为原始材料还好好地躺在那儿；而你从没记下来的材料是真没了 —— 三个月前那次对话、某个决定背后的理由、客户说过他讨厌什么。一种失败可以挽回，另一种不能，而人们偏偏最先纠结那个可以挽回的。所以做那个不性感的版本：现在什么都往里倒，结构的事以后从这堆东西里自己长出来。

## B3. Context Is a Retrieval Problem, and That Reframes What "More" Means

**EN**
It is tempting to treat context as a volume problem: collect more, and the model gets better. That is wrong in a way that quietly wastes a lot of effort. What the model needs at any moment is not your total context but the slice relevant to the task in front of it, and dumping the whole pile in does not help — it dilutes the relevant part with everything else. Once you see it that way, the goal of accumulating changes shape. You are not building a bigger archive; you are building one where any given piece can be pulled out on demand. This is also why accumulating and distilling have to be separate steps rather than one tidy workflow: distilling is what makes the material addressable. It is the index, not the compression. And it is why the same problem shows up everywhere in tooling — a hybrid search over your own notes, semantic plus keyword, is just an answer to the question of which slice of context this task needs.

> **中**
> 人们很容易把上下文当成一个体积问题：收集得越多，模型就越好。这个错法会悄悄浪费掉大量精力。模型在任何一刻需要的都不是你的全部上下文，而是眼前这个任务相关的那一片，而把整堆东西全倒进去并不会更好 —— 它只会用其余的一切稀释掉相关的部分。一旦这样看，积累的目标就变了形：你不是在建一个更大的档案库，你是在建一个"任何一块都能按需取出来"的库。这也是为什么积累和提炼必须分成两步、而不是合并成一个整洁的工作流 —— 提炼的作用正是让材料变得可寻址。它是索引，不是压缩。同样的问题在工具里到处都是：对自己笔记做语义加关键词的混合检索，无非就是在回答"这个任务需要哪一片上下文"。

# Part C · Extras (English Only — Chinese Folded for Self-Check)

## C1. Sorting Advice by Whether It Lives in Information or in Time

*Terms: **table stakes** = the minimum everyone must have just to compete; **inflate away** = lose value over time; **elapsed time** = time that has actually passed.*

Here is the reusable move, stated once so you can apply it to any list of recommendations. Take the advice you were given and ask, for each item, what the input actually is. If the input is knowledge — a tool name, a prompt, a framework — then it lives in information, and it will be copied out to everyone within months. Learn it, use it, and do not build an identity on it. If the input is elapsed time — accumulated context, a body of work, a relationship, a reputation — then it lives in time, and no one can shortcut it. That is where the compounding is, and it is also where the uncomfortable part sits: the return is nearly invisible for a long stretch before it turns. Most people quit during the flat part, which is precisely why the ones who do not quit end up with something nobody can copy. Two categories, one question, and you will know where your next hundred hours belong.

## C2. The Unfair Test for Any Prompt You Write

*Terms: **hold output to a standard** = judge results against an explicit bar; **ship** = finalise and start using; **tweak the tone** = fiddle with style rather than substance.*

One more thing worth carrying out of this video, because it takes thirty seconds and catches most bad prompts. Before you ship a prompt, read it looking for three specific things. Does it describe a process — what happens first, what happens next, what happens when something fails? Does it name a tool — something the model can actually call, rather than merely describe? Does it state a standard — what counts as a good answer, and what should be rejected? A prompt with all three is an interface, and interfaces survive contact with new tasks. A prompt with none of them is a mood board: it will produce something pleasant, and it will not produce anything twice. The failure is easy to miss because the output looks good. Good-looking output is not the test; repeatable output is the test.
