# Part B · 论证展开（整段英文 → 整段中文）
# Part B · The Argument in Full (English Paragraph First)

## B1. Why "Handbook or Not" Is the Step Everyone Skips

**EN**
Most people start distilling by throwing a file at the model and asking for a summary, and that is precisely where it goes wrong. A summary treats every book as the same object, but books are not the same object, and the difference determines what is worth extracting. A practical handbook contains a method: a sequence of moves that produces a result, stated explicitly enough that someone else can run it. Distill that and you get something you can execute the next time the problem shows up. A book of essays or narrative history contains no such sequence, and if you ask for one the model will manufacture it — you will receive confident, well-formatted steps that the author never endorsed. What those books actually contain is a way of looking, a set of questions the author reaches for in a particular order. Distill that instead and you get a scaffold: not an answer, but a reliable way of generating one. Same input pipeline, two completely different outputs, and the only thing that decides which one you get is a classification you make before you start.

> **中**
> 大多数人做蒸馏，是把一个文件丢给模型然后要一份摘要，问题恰恰就出在这里。摘要把每一本书都当成同一种东西，但书并不是同一种东西，而正是这个差别决定了什么值得提取。实用型的工具书里有一套方法论：一串能产生结果的动作，明确到别人照着也能跑。蒸馏它，你得到的是下次问题出现时可以直接执行的东西。而一本随笔或叙事性的历史著作里并没有这样的动作序列；你硬要，模型就会给你编 —— 你会拿到一堆自信、格式工整、但作者从未背书过的步骤。这类书真正含着的是一种看法，是作者会按某种次序取出的一串问题。改去蒸馏这个，你得到的是一个脚手架：不是答案，而是一种稳定地产出答案的方式。同一条输入管道，两种完全不同的产出，而决定你拿到哪一种的，只有你在动手之前做的那一次分类。

## B2. Generalising the Test Is the Move That Actually Pays

**EN**
Here is the part worth stealing, and it is one sentence in the video. Once you notice that books are only one member of a larger set, the usable version of the rule stops being "distill books" and becomes "distill anything that yields a method or a thinking frame". That reformulation is worth more than the tutorial around it, because it is what lets you apply the same pipeline to a paper you read last week, to the notes from a meeting you barely remember, to a podcast transcript, or to your own post-mortem on a project that went badly. Notice what the reformulation throws away: it does not care about length, format, prestige, or whether the thing was ever published. It cares about exactly one property — can this material be turned into something I can reach for the next time I hit a problem? If yes, it is raw material for a skill. If it is merely large and impressive, it is reference material, and reference material is what search is for. That is the whole test, and it takes about five seconds to run.

> **中**
> 这里才是真正值得偷走的部分，而视频里它只有一句话。一旦你意识到"书"只是一个更大集合里的一个成员，这条规则可用的版本就不再是什么"蒸馏书"，而是"蒸馏任何能产出方法论或思维框架的东西"。这次改写比它外面那整篇教程都值钱，因为正是它让你能把同一条管道套到上周读的一篇论文上、套到一场你快记不清的会议纪要上、套到一份播客逐字稿上，或者套到你自己给一个搞砸的项目写的复盘上。注意这次改写扔掉了什么：它不在乎篇幅、格式、名气，也不在乎这东西有没有出版过。它只在乎一个属性 —— 这份材料能不能变成"下次我撞上问题时能伸手拿到的东西"？能，它就是 Skill 的原料。如果它只是又大又唬人，那它是参考资料，而参考资料是交给检索的。这就是全部的判据，跑一遍大约五秒钟。

## B3. Why the Fourth Step Is the One That Matters

**EN**
The four steps look like a production line, but they are not evenly weighted, and the weighting is the interesting part. Steps one through three produce an artefact: you did a task, the model described the workflow, and the valuable slice of it got written down as a skill. That artefact is useful on the day it is written and slightly less useful every day after, because the tools around it change, the task drifts, and the assumptions baked into it quietly expire. Step four is what breaks that curve, and it does it with one instruction — give the skill a way to grow. Now every use is also a round of tuning: the skill encounters a case it handled badly, adjusts, and carries the adjustment forward. A template is a snapshot of your understanding on one afternoon; an asset is something that compounds. The question to carry out of this video is not "how do I write a skill" but "does the thing I just wrote get better every time I use it?" If the answer is no, you have produced a document, and documents need maintenance.

> **中**
> 这四步看起来像一条流水线，但它们的权重并不均匀，而权重才是真正有意思的地方。第一到第三步产出的是一个制品：你做了一件任务，模型描述了工作流，其中最有价值的那一片被写成了一个 Skill。这个制品在写完那天很好用，之后每过一天就稍微不好用一点，因为它周围的工具在变、任务在漂移，而当初写进去的那些假设正在悄悄过期。第四步正是打断这条曲线的东西，而它只用了一条指令 —— 给这个 Skill 一种成长的方式。于是每一次使用同时也变成了一轮调优：Skill 撞上一个自己处理得很糟的场景，做出调整，然后把这次调整带进下一次。模板是你某一个下午的理解的一张快照；资产是会复利的东西。从这条视频里该带走的那个问题不是"我怎么写一个 Skill"，而是"我刚写的这个东西，会不会越用越好"。如果答案是不会，那你产出的是一份文档，而文档是需要维护的。

## C1. Matching the Tool to the Stage You Are Actually At

*Terms: **burden of judgement** = the requirement that you supply the judgement yourself; **upstream** = earlier in the pipeline, before the main step; **degrade** = get worse, usually quietly.*

There is a broader lesson hiding in the tool comparison, and it applies well beyond distilling. Heavy tools tend to push their requirements upstream: they assume you arrive with the judgement already made, and they spend their power on structure rather than on interpretation. That is a good trade when you have the judgement, because it stops the tool from second-guessing you. It is a terrible trade when you do not, because the tool will now structure your confusion with total confidence, and a well-organised version of a wrong reading is harder to spot than a messy one. So the honest question before adopting any powerful tool is not "what can this do" but "what does this quietly assume I already have". If you cannot supply the assumption, you do not need a weaker tool out of laziness — you need one that closes the gap for you, and you can switch later. Tools are not ladders you climb once; they are gear you swap as the terrain changes.

## C2. The Five-Second Test for Whether Something Is Worth Distilling

*Terms: **raw material** = input suitable for processing; **reach for** = grab instinctively when needed; **reference material** = stuff you look up rather than execute.*

One last thing, because it turns a two-hour decision into a five-second one. Before you distill anything, ask what the finished artefact is supposed to do the next time you have a problem. If the honest answer is "I would run its steps", you are distilling a method, and the source has to actually contain one. If the answer is "I would think the way this person thinks", you are distilling a frame, and the source only needs to display that way of thinking consistently. If the answer is neither — if you cannot picture yourself reaching for it — then the material is not raw material, however valuable it is. Put it in your reference pile and let search find it later. Distilling is effort spent once against a payoff that arrives repeatedly, so it belongs only on things you will actually reach for again and again.
