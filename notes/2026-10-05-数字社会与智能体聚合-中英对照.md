## B1. Why a One-Way Door Changes How You Should Rank Your Effort

**EN**
There is a habit of treating digital migration as one trend among many, which is exactly how people miss what is unusual about it. A trend you can join late is a trend where waiting costs you time. A one-way door is different: waiting costs you the position itself, and there is no later in which to make it up. The argument for irreversibility here is almost embarrassingly simple. Nobody who has moved online announces that they are done with the internet; a handful manage it, and the species does not. So the migration completes once, in one direction, and the ordering inside it is what matters — whoever moves first ends up holding the view from above, and everyone else arrives into a world already arranged around them. That gives you a rule you can actually apply to your own week. Separate the things you are putting off into two piles: things that are recoverable later, and things that can only be done once. Then do the second pile first, even when the first pile looks more urgent. Reorganisation, refactoring, redesign — all recoverable, because the raw material is still sitting there. Migration, capture, showing up early — not recoverable, and precisely the ones that feel skippable.

> **中**
> 人们习惯把数字化迁移当成众多趋势中的一个，而恰恰是这种看法让人错过了它真正不寻常的地方。一个可以晚加入的趋势，等待的代价只是时间；一扇单向门则不同 —— 等待的代价是那个位置本身，而且不存在"以后再补上"这回事。这里关于不可逆性的论证简单得近乎直白：没有一个已经转到线上的人会宣布自己从此不再用线上；极少数人做得到，人类这个物种做不到。所以这场迁移只发生一次、只有一个方向，而真正重要的是迁移内部的先后次序 —— 先转的人最终拿到了俯视的视角，其他所有人抵达时，世界已经围绕他们安排好了。这就给了你一条真正能用在自己这一周上的规则：把你正在拖延的事情分成两堆 —— 以后还能补的，和只能做一次的；然后先做第二堆，哪怕第一堆看起来更紧急。重组、重构、重新设计 —— 全都可挽回，因为原始材料还好好地躺在那儿。迁移、记录、早到场 —— 不可挽回，而偏偏就是这几件最容易让人觉得"可以往后放"。

## B2. Information and Influence Are Two Different Asymmetries, and That Distinction Is the Whole Point

**EN**
It is tempting to collapse the two asymmetries into one complaint — the platform knows more than I do — and doing so hides the most useful part of the argument. Knowing more is not the same as being able to bargain. A platform can hand you every scrap of data it has and still refuse to move on price, because your leverage as a single person has not changed by one gram. That is why the video dismantles them separately, and why the order matters. The first asymmetry falls to the agent: an agent that never sleeps and can roam the whole network sees what the platform sees, so the informational gap closes on its own. The second one does not fall to better information at all; it falls to aggregation. Ten thousand separate requests are ten thousand people who can be ignored. One request representing a hundred thousand pressure cookers is a negotiation. The practical consequence is worth stating plainly, because it is the kind of thing that gets misremembered: giving individuals better tools removes the information gap, but unless those individuals can also combine, the balance of power has not moved. Information symmetry is a precondition. Aggregation is what actually changes the price.

> **中**
> 人们很容易把两条不对称压缩成一句抱怨 —— 平台知道得比我多 —— 而这么一压，就把论证里最有用的部分藏起来了。知道得多，并不等于能讨价还价。平台可以把它掌握的每一丝数据都给你，然后照样在价格上一步不让，因为你作为单个人的议价能力连一克都没有变。这正是视频要把它们分开拆解的原因，也正是顺序之所以重要的原因。第一条不对称是被智能体消掉的：一个不睡觉、能跑遍全网的智能体，看到的东西和平台看到的一样，于是信息差自己就闭合了。第二条不对称根本不是靠更好的信息消掉的，它是靠聚合消掉的。一万个分散的请求，就是一万个可以被无视的人；一个代表十万口高压锅的请求，是一场谈判。由此得出的实际结论值得直说，因为它恰恰是最容易被记错的地方：给个体更好的工具，消掉的是信息差；但除非这些个体还能联合起来，力量对比并没有移动分毫。信息对称是前提，聚合才是真正改变价格的东西。

## B3. Group Buying vs. Aggregation: A Test You Can Run on Any Feature

**EN**
The pressure cooker example is worth carrying out of the video, because it is not really about cookers. It is a test for any feature that needs a crowd to work. Look at how your product gathers demand and ask one question: what is doing the coordinating? If the answer is a social relationship — invite your friends, share it to a group, get three people to join — then you have built something that spends human goodwill every time it runs, and whose ceiling is the size of one person's social circle. It also quietly forces people to want things they do not want, which is the part everyone notices and nobody fixes. If the answer is the demand itself — a place where needs are posted, a heartbeat that checks it, and a mechanism that merges matching needs into one block — then you have built something that spends nothing, scales to the whole network, and never asks anybody to pretend. The second version was not possible before, and not because nobody thought of it. It needed an actor that could post, check, and combine on your behalf without you being present, and that actor arrived recently. So the reusable move is this: find the places in your product where a user is currently being asked to recruit other users, and ask whether a machine could do the recruiting instead.

> **中**
> 高压锅这个例子值得从视频里带走，因为它其实跟高压锅没关系。它是一道检验题，可以套用在任何"必须凑够一堆人才成立"的功能上。看看你的产品是怎么聚拢需求的，然后问一个问题：真正在做协调的是什么？如果答案是社交关系 —— 邀请好友、分享到群里、凑够三个人 —— 那你做出来的东西每跑一次就消耗一次人情，而且它的上限就是一个人社交圈的大小。它还会悄悄逼着人去要一些自己根本不想要的东西，这一点所有人都感觉到了，却没人修。如果答案是需求本身 —— 有一个地方可以贴需求、有一个心跳定期去查、有一套机制把同类需求并成一块 —— 那你做出来的东西不消耗任何东西、可以放大到全网、而且从不需要任何人装样子。第二个版本以前做不成，原因不是没人想到过：它需要一个能替你发帖、替你查看、替你合并、而你不必在场的角色，而这个角色是最近才到的。所以可复用的动作是这样：找出你的产品里那些"正在要求用户去拉别的用户"的地方，然后问一句 —— 这件事能不能交给机器去做。

## C1. Two Rulers for Any Platform You Meet

*Terms: **God's-eye view** = the position of seeing everything because you sit above it; **asymmetry** = one side has more of something than the other; **leverage** = the ability to change someone else's behaviour.*

Here is the portable version of this video, in the form of two rulers you can lay against any platform you use or build. Ruler one: information. Ask what the platform knows that the individual user does not, and be specific — not "data" but which data, about whom, and what decision it changes. Ruler two: influence. Ask in which direction influence actually flows, because in most platforms it only flows one way. Write both answers down and you have a description of the moat that is more honest than anything in a pitch deck, and you also have a map of where the moat is thin: any asymmetry that technology is currently eroding is a place where the platform is weaker than it looks. One caution about using these rulers. Do not assume that closing the information gap also closes the influence gap — that is the single most common misreading of this argument, and it leads people to believe that giving users better tools is sufficient. It is necessary and it is not sufficient. Ask the second question separately, every time.

## C2. The Life-Support Test

*Terms: **old wine in new bottles** = the same thing repackaged to look new; **on life support** = kept alive artificially rather than actually healthy; **set aside** = deliberately put out of consideration.*

The last twenty-five seconds of the video are the part worth remembering, because they turn into a test you can run in about a minute on anything you are building. Take the thing in front of you and ask what it would look like if the new technology had never arrived. If the answer is "roughly the same, but slower", you are almost certainly pouring old wine into new bottles: the technology is being used to keep an existing process alive rather than to ask whether the process should exist. The reason this failure is hard to catch is that the result usually works. It ships, it demos well, and users can tell that something advanced is happening underneath, which is exactly why nobody asks the harder question. The harder question is this: under conditions where the technology is fully available, what would a person actually ask for? Notice that this is not the same as asking what they would ask for today. People answer the second question using the constraints they currently live inside, and the whole point of a mature technology is that those constraints are no longer real. So ask it as a blank-slate question, then compare your answer to what you are building. If the two match, you were already thinking in the new conditions. If they do not, you now know what the gap costs.

## 落地清单（中文速览 / 无英文）

按"先做什么最划算"排序：

1. **用两把尺子量一个平台** —— 它比我知道得多在哪？它能影响我而我在哪影响不了它？两个答案就是它的护城河，也是它最薄的地方
2. **别把两条不对称合成一条** —— 看得一样全 ≠ 能议价；信息对称只是前提，真正改变价格的是聚合出来的量
3. **遇到"凑够一堆人才成立"的功能，问一句谁在做协调** —— 靠社交关系凑就消耗人情、上限是社交圈；靠需求本身聚合就不消耗、能放大到全网
4. **列出产品里"要求用户去拉用户"的地方** —— 逐条问：这件事能不能交给机器做
5. **做一次续命自检** —— 如果这项技术从未出现，你做的东西会是什么样？答案是"差不多只是更慢"，就是新瓶装旧酒
6. **用不可逆性排优先级** —— 能补的往后放，只能做一次的先做，哪怕它看起来不紧急
