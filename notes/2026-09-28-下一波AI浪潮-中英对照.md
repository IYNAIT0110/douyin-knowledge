**EN** Even for OpenAI, it's like we train models and then we discover their capabilities.

> 就是说，benchmark 并不会告诉你全部。

**EN** We have to play quite a bit with the models themselves to sort of realize, like, oh, you know, maybe we haven't thought about benefiting from it in like this specific way.
> 我们得大量地把玩这些模型本身，才会慢慢意识到：哦，原来我们还没想过可以这样用它。

## A2. 笔记本本身成了约束 / The Laptop Becomes the Constraint

**EN** Another thing that, you know, you realize when you have very, very powerful models is that your laptop kind of becomes a constraint in and by itself.
> 还有一件事，当你有了非常非常强的模型时就会意识到：你的笔记本本身就成了一种约束。

**EN** All these things are human constraints. The model doesn't have the same constraints.
> 这些都是人类约束。模型并不受同样的约束。

## A4. 递归自我改进的真正形态 / What Recursive Self-Improvement Actually Looks Like

**EN** Recursive self-improvement is obviously a huge topic right now and it's most often, I think, applied to research and models developing other models.
> 递归自我改进显然眼下是个大话题，而它最常被用在研究上，也就是"模型造模型"。

**EN** But what we are seeing a ton of success with is using those models to develop the infrastructure that is on the critical path of using those models, which is also a form of recursive self-improvement.
> 但我们看到大量成功的是另一件事：用这些模型去开发**位于"使用这些模型的关键路径上"的基础设施** —— 这也是一种递归自我改进。

**EN** So it's all one big system.
> 所以这就是一个大系统。

**EN** And so of course we're doing that. Like if we were not doing that I think that would be pretty silly.
> 所以我们当然在做。要是我们不做，那才叫蠢。

**EN** There's very little stop energy in general for like new product ideas, which is exhilarating and fun.
> 总的来说，对新点子几乎没有"叫停的能量"，这让人兴奋，也很好玩。

## B1 · 文化：自下而上，但要有对偶面（约 204–311s）

**EN** OpenAI has a very bottom-up culture. It's a very empowering culture. People can come up with all sorts of ideas, get together, and then very, very quickly ship something. There's very little stop energy in general for new product ideas, which is exhilarating and fun. And so preserving that is very important to me. The other thing that is also important is to not make it a mess. You don't want to have a hodgepodge of features and no overall direction and coherence. And so it's counterbalanced with the sense of simplicity, and with you being proud about the quality of the product. So there are these overall principles, while still empowering everyone to try new things and ship very quickly.
> OpenAI 的文化非常自下而上，也非常赋权。人们可以想出各种各样的点子，凑到一起，然后非常非常快地把东西发出去。总的来说，对新点子的"拦停力"很小 —— 这让人兴奋，也好玩。所以保住这一点对我来说非常重要。另一件同样重要的事是别把它搞成一团乱麻 —— 你不会想要一堆拼凑的功能，却没有整体方向和一致性。所以要用"简洁感"、用"你对产品质量感到自豪"去制衡它。于是就有了这些总体原则，同时又仍然赋权给每个人去尝试新东西、快速发布。

**EN** So I think having conviction, finding a way to have users and iterate very quickly from feedback, and also being willing to disrupt yourself. We come up with new research, new ideas all the time, and being able to identify when is the right moment to go and invest in them, even though it means maybe reallocating resources from the main gig. It's very hard, but it's super important to be able to do that.
> 所以我认为：要有信念，要找到办法拿到用户、并根据反馈极快地迭代，还要愿意**颠覆自己**。我们一直在产出新研究、新想法，关键是能判断出**什么时候**该去投入它们 —— 哪怕这意味着要从主业里把资源挪走。这非常难，但能做到这一点极其重要。

## B2 · 笔记本本身成了约束（约 536–580s）

**EN** What you realize when you have very, very powerful models is that your laptop kind of becomes a constraint in and by itself. The amount of work that you can do on a laptop — it was designed for humans, right? So it's designed roughly to be able to absorb the amount of work that you can produce: how fast you can type, how fast you can think, how many applications you need open. All these things are human constraints. The model doesn't have the same constraints. The model can, for example, handle a hundred applications opened at the same time perfectly fine, maybe in the future. And so in terms of access to resources, it's very clear that models of the future will need access to more than the resource of your laptop.
> 当你手里有了非常非常强的模型，你会发现：你的笔记本本身就变成了一种约束。你在笔记本上能干多少活 —— 它可是**为人**设计的，对吧？所以它被设计成大致刚好能吸收"你能产出"的工作量：你打字多快、你想得多快、你需要同时开多少个应用。这些全都是**人的约束**。模型没有同样的约束。比如模型可以同时开着一百个应用而毫无问题 —— 也许在未来就是这样。所以从资源获取的角度看，有一点是清楚的：未来的模型需要的资源，会超出你一台笔记本能给的那点资源。

## B3 · 瓶颈换地方了：工具调用成为新的带宽（约 600–672s）

**EN** When you have token speeds that are — I think 14 is the stated number — 14 times faster than what fast is, the bandwidth constraint changes. The CPU now becomes the bandwidth: literally tool calls. Network, tool call, any kind of overhead in the stack becomes the limiting factor. But then you can compensate by doing multiple things concurrently. So you can think about exploring on one end, writing tests as well, compiling, testing a new hypothesis, all at once. And so then you're shifting the bottleneck around, because you're able to do more concurrently.
> 当你的 token 速度达到 —— 我记得公开的数字是 14 —— 比"fast"快 14 倍时，**带宽约束就变了**。这时候 CPU 成了带宽，字面意义上的"工具调用"成了带宽。网络、工具调用，技术栈里任何一种开销，都会变成限制因素。但你可以通过并发做多件事来补偿。于是你可以一边探索，一边写测试、编译、验证一个新假设 —— 全都在同时进行。所以你其实是在**把瓶颈挪来挪去**，因为你能并发干的事变多了。

**EN** With current token speeds, I find myself kicking off 10, 15 agents in parallel. And that becomes a pretty significant cognitive overhead for me, doing that context switching — because you're kicking it off and you can expect 30, 45 minutes before my task comes back. Now, with ultra-fast speed, that workflow changes significantly. And I don't think I would be able to have 10 or 15 agents. And that might be a good thing — maybe it's three or four at a time.
> 按现在的 token 速度，我发现自己会同时起 10 个、15 个 agent。而这给我带来了相当大的认知开销 —— 光是来回切换上下文就很累，因为你把任务丢出去之后，得等 30 到 45 分钟它才回来。现在有了 ultra-fast 的速度，这套工作流发生了明显变化。我觉得我不再会同时开 10 个或 15 个 agent 了。而这也许是件好事 —— 也许一次三四个就够了。

## B4 · 注意力友好：让技术适应你（约 677–743s）

**EN** I think managing your attention and being much more friendly to your attention is something that we care a lot about. After all, we're trying to build for humans. We're trying to build the technology that's the most empowering for humans. And that requires building around your ability to multitask, and how you want to manage your attention. Do you want something brought up now? Or is it better to bring it up in 30 minutes?
> 我认为，**管理你的注意力、对注意力更友好**，是我们非常在意的事。毕竟我们是在为人而造。我们在试图造出对人类最有赋权感的技术。而这要求我们围绕"你的多任务能力"、围绕"你想怎么管理注意力"来构建。你是想现在就被提示某件事？还是 30 分钟后再提更好？

**EN** And then when you have ultra-fast speeds combined, maybe with voice, suddenly you're like: okay, this thing can operate at the same speed, if not faster than you. And so you stay in the flow. You get to ideate, you get to see prototypes, you get to build little reports in real time. Suddenly you're like: oh yeah, what I was doing before — multitasking like 10 agents — I don't really want to go back to that. And so we're trying to bring that sort of experience, that it's just really natural, but also feels built for you, and where you don't have to adapt — the technology adapts to you.
> 然后当超快的速度再配上（比如说）语音，你会突然发现：好吧，这东西的运行速度和你一样快，甚至比你更快。于是你就能一直待在心流里。你可以构思、可以看到原型、可以实时地做出小报告。然后你会突然意识到：哦对，我以前那种干法 —— 同时 multitask 十个 agent —— 我真不太想回去了。所以我们想带来的就是这种体验：它非常自然，同时又让你觉得"这是为我造的"，而且**不需要你去适应它 —— 是技术来适应你**。

## B5 · 两类问题：个人 AGI 与全面自动化（约 787–870s）

**EN** So I think about two different categories of problems. Like the first one is building the very best personal AGI or the personal agent that will be in the flow with you, proactive, raise important new ideas when it can find some, be very, very efficient at doing exactly what you want. It doesn't matter whether it's a technical problem or, you know, it's just more like research or advice, like you can do it all. And it's like super, super tailored to you. This is a very important thing. And it's like deeply rooted in the understanding of you as a human, you as an individual that is unique. That's one category of problem. The other category of problems is like full on automation, you know, where you're more building intelligent systems that can take care of a very complex process. For example, you know, going and looking at production logs and automatically doing performance optimizations, or looking at regressions and automatically patching them. Without a human in the loop, or like, you know, very, very minimal where you only need to approve a high risk action.
> 我会把它想成两类不同的问题。第一类，是造出最好的"个人 AGI"或者说个人 agent —— 它跟你在同一个心流里，能主动，能在发现苗头时提出重要的新想法，并且非常非常高效地做你真正想做的事。它是技术问题还是别的都不重要，可能更像是研究，或者咨询 —— 它都能干。而且它是极度、极度为你定制的。这件事非常重要，它深深植根于对你的理解：把你当作一个人，当作一个独一无二的人。这是第一类问题。另一类问题是全面自动化 —— 你在造的是那种能接管一个非常复杂流程的智能系统。比如说，去翻生产日志，自动做性能优化；或者发现 regression 就自动把补丁打上。人在环外，或者参与度极低 —— 只需要你审批高风险动作。

## B6 · Codex 与 ChatGPT 合流：界面按个体自适应（约 886–1001s）

**EN** It's really been a boon. So the feedback we had initially was like: why do you merge them? Do you really have to do it? And it's like: well, the future — our future models want us to be merged. So we're just going to do it, because it is the simple and proper thing to do, where we're building this very personal, super capable agent that can help you in all sorts of ways. This is going to be the same technology under the hood. It's the same harness. It's the same way that we think about it. It's highly multimodal, voice first, super efficient. And it doesn't matter if you're trying to code or not — this agent is capable of it all.
> 这确实是一件大好事。我们最初收到的反馈是：你们为什么要合并？真的非合不可吗？而答案是：未来 —— 我们未来的模型需要我们合并。所以我们就是要做，因为我们在造的这个"非常个人化、能力超强的 agent"，合并才是简单而正确的做法。底层会是同一套技术，同一个 harness，同一套思考方式。它是高度多模态的、语音优先的、极其高效的。不管你是不是在写代码，都无所谓 —— 这个 agent 全都干得了。

**EN** And then the interface that you want is that it should tailor itself to your needs. You shouldn't decide: I'm a coder, I want a coder interface, or I'm not technical, I want a non-technical interface. There's a spectrum of people. We come up with labels like a software engineer, a designer — these are just human concepts that we have invented to deal with abstractions, because the reality is too complex for us to handle. But individuals are individual: they're somewhere on the spectrum. And so we're trying to build the perfect interface that adapts for everyone. It doesn't matter if you're technical or not. It adapts based on your specific individuality.
> 而且你想要的界面，应该是会**按你的需要自我裁剪**的那种。你不该被迫先做选择：我是个程序员，我要程序员界面；或者我不懂技术，我要非技术界面。人是分布在一个**谱系**上的。我们发明出"软件工程师""设计师"这样的标签 —— 这些不过是人类为了处理抽象而造出来的概念，因为现实太复杂，我们处理不过来。但每个个体就是个体，他处在谱系上的某个位置。所以我们在试图造出一个能为所有人自适应的完美界面。你懂不懂技术都不重要 —— 它按你**这个具体的人**来自适应。

**EN** It's kind of wild to think that my mom might use the same exact interface as me. And then obviously it'll customize to my needs. Maybe I'll need more information if I'm doing more sophisticated work. … That's right, it's the same thing. So you and your mom will use the same thing. It will be your personal AGI.
> 想想还挺奇妙的：我妈可能会用跟我**一模一样**的界面。然后显然它会按我的需要去定制 —— 如果我在做更复杂的工作，也许我需要看到更多信息。……没错，就是这样。所以你和你妈会用同一个东西。而它就是你的 personal AGI。

# 文末速览清单（中文）

- 能力是**玩**出来的：benchmark 只给下限，真实用法增量来自拿自己的任务去撞。
- **笔记本是为人类设计的**，模型不受同样的约束 → 下一代模型需要的资源多于一笔记本。
- 速度提上去后，瓶颈从生成转移到 **tool calls / 网络 / CPU**；工具调用密集场景只有 3-4 倍，不是 14 倍。
- **递归自我改进**最实在的形态：用模型去优化**跑模型的关键路径**（kernel、serving、调度），然后把收益分享出去。
- 组织能力 = **愿意颠覆自己**：难在从主业抽调资源，不在判断。
- 快速 ship 要用"简洁感和质量自豪感"来**平衡**，否则就成一锅粥。
- **标签（工程师／设计师）只是人类为应付抽象发明的概念** —— 界面该按个体自适应，不按角色分叉。
- 采用率的预测器不是"功能多强"，而是**阻力有多小**。
- 对担心 AI 的人：他不讲大道理，讲效率复利 + 已经发生的私人用途 + "让你见医生时更有准备"。
